📜 Historical Development of the Indian Constitution

The Constitution of India did not emerge suddenly in 1949. It evolved over nearly two centuries through a series of constitutional developments during British rule. The British Parliament passed several Acts to regulate the administration of India. Many of the administrative principles introduced through these Acts later became the foundation of the Constitution of India.

The constitutional development can broadly be divided into three phases:

  1. Company Rule (1773–1858) – Administration by the East India Company.
  2. Crown Rule (1858–1947) – Administration directly by the British Crown.
  3. Independent India (1947 onwards) – Framing and implementation of the Constitution.

🎯 Dy.E.O. Focus

Remember the sequence of constitutional Acts. Most competitive examinations ask either the chronological order, important provisions, or comparison between two Acts.

📜 Regulating Act, 1773

Background

The East India Company had become politically powerful after the Battle of Buxar (1764). Corruption, mismanagement and financial difficulties forced the British Parliament to regulate the Company's administration in India.

Major Provisions

  • First Act passed by the British Parliament to control the East India Company.
  • Governor of Bengal became the Governor-General of Bengal.
  • Warren Hastings became the first Governor-General of Bengal.
  • An Executive Council of four members was created.
  • The Supreme Court was established at Calcutta in 1774.
  • The Governors of Bombay and Madras became subordinate to Bengal in important matters.
  • The Company's servants were prohibited from accepting gifts and engaging in private trade.

⭐ Exam Alert

Remember these three points:

  • First Parliamentary Control
  • First Governor-General of Bengal
  • Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774)

🧠 Memory Trick

CONTROL → GOVERNOR → COURT

Whenever you think of the Regulating Act, immediately remember:

  1. Parliamentary Control
  2. Governor-General
  3. Supreme Court

📜 Pitt's India Act, 1784

Background

The Regulating Act failed to solve administrative problems completely. The British Government therefore passed Pitt's India Act to establish better control over Indian administration.

Major Provisions

  • Established Dual Government (Dual Control).
  • Created the Board of Control.
  • The Court of Directors continued to manage commercial affairs.
  • The British Government obtained complete political control.
  • Distinguished commercial functions from political functions.

🎯 Dy.E.O. Focus

Whenever you see Pitt's India Act, think immediately of Dual Control.

🧠 Memory Trick

PITT = TWO BOSSES

Board of Control + Court of Directors
